Confirm the environment, permitted tools, expected artifact and scoring. Choose practice that reproduces those constraints.

## Problem

A fictional recruiter packet confirms two rounds:

| Round | Environment/time | Tool rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Implementation | Existing codebase, 45 minutes | One named embedded assistant allowed |
| Oral math | Spoken derivation | No AI |

Choose the drills. Does the first permission authorize an outside assistant or tools in the second round?

## Work the Packet

1. Practice code navigation, small edits, tests and review within 45 minutes using only the authorized assistant.
2. Practice closed-book derivations and changed-assumption follow-ups for oral math.
3. Do not transfer permission between tools or rounds. Clarify any conflict with public policy before assessment.

## The preparation decision tree

Ask the recruiter six questions:

1. What are the exact round names and durations?
2. Is implementation blank-file, codebase, notebook, take-home, or trace-based?
3. Which AI tools are allowed in each round?
4. Is there a presentation, research discussion, or artifact review?
5. Which domain and team will technical depth target?
6. What does each round produce or score?

Then map format to drill:

- unfamiliar codebase plus AI: [agentic codebase lab](/prep/labs/agentic-codebase/);
- LLM training debugging: [broken training lab](/prep/labs/broken-training/);
- research notebook or API: [black-box lab](/prep/labs/black-box/);
- project presentation: [presentation protocol](/prep/presentation/);
- performance trace: [accelerator lab](/prep/labs/accelerator/);
- ML primitives: [implementation set](/prep/labs/implementation/);
- oral math: [math lab](/prep/labs/math-oral/);
- values or mission: [values packet](/prep/values/).

## Change 1: One Round Is Silent

**New task.** A second implementation round gives no tool rule.

**Picture and change.** Permission belongs to a round/tool pair, not the whole loop. Ask before using tools.

**Check.** The first round's allowance does not propagate.

**Use when.** Instructions differ or are incomplete.

## Change 2: A Report Describes a Pilot

**New task.** A dated account describes a new format.

**Picture and change.** Keep its role/date scope; use it to ask about your round, not assert a stable process.

**Check.** An AI-use questionnaire, coding assistant, reported pilot and AI grading are four different claims.

**Use when.** Official detail is sparse.

## What not to do

Do not route by brand, practice every lab or memorize alleged leaked questions.
Jobs describe work scope, not exact prompts. Fellows or pilot evidence stays within that population.

## Evidence quality matters

Separate five evidence classes:

1. Official general policy: organization-wide context, not every round's detail.
2. Current role/round instructions: scoped constraints; clarify conflicts with an authorized contact.
3. Method-described reports: inspect recency and population.
4. First-person accounts: apply only to that candidate's role/date.
5. Unverified anecdotes: questions to ask, not facts or permission.

## Source Record

Record company, team/role, round, format, tools, artifact, duration, scoring, source URL, evidence class, checked-at date, scope and caveat.
Unknown is valid; resolve consequential gaps before using tools.
