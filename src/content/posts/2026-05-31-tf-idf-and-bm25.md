## Summary

Lexical scoring combines analyzed token matches with corpus statistics, not a direct measurement of relevance.
BM25 separates diminishing frequency gain from document-length normalization; TF-IDF uses a declared weighting and vector-normalization convention.
Neither lexical, dense, nor fused retrieval is guaranteed to win every query slice.

## Term frequency and its problem

Raw counts can be replaced with damped weights, such as $1+\log f$ for positive frequency and zero otherwise.
This heuristic limits growth relative to raw count; it does not prove that repeated words are relevant.
The log-TF weight is still unbounded, unlike BM25's fixed-length frequency factor.

## Inverse document frequency

One unsmoothed TF-IDF convention uses natural-log inverse document frequency:

$$
\text{idf}(t) = \log \frac{N}{\text{df}(t)},
$$

Here $1\le\operatorname{df}(t)\le N$ counts indexed documents containing the analyzed term.
Dot-product sums and cosine-normalized TF-IDF vectors define different scorers; state which is intended.

## BM25: the production formula

For $k_1>0$, $0\le b\le1$, and positive average field length, use:

$$
\text{BM25}(q, d) = \sum_{t \in q} \text{idf}(t) \cdot \frac{f(t,d)\,(k_1 + 1)}{f(t,d) + k_1\big(1 - b + b\,\frac{|d|}{\text{avgdl}}\big)},
$$

- $f(t,d)$ counts term occurrences; $|d|$ and $avgdl$ use the index's field-length convention.
- $k_1$ controls saturation; the displayed factor approaches $k_1+1$ at fixed length.
- $b$ controls the length adjustment; $b=0$ removes it.

Sum each distinct analyzed query term once here; repeated-query weighting is a separate convention.
An absent term contributes zero, including empty fields.
Robertson's IDF $\log((N-df+0.5)/(df+0.5))$ can be negative.
Lucene's log-one-plus variant is $\log(1+(N-df+0.5)/(df+0.5))$ and stays positive for indexed terms.

Fix $k_1=1.2$, $b=0.75$, and positive IDF one.
At average length, frequencies $1,3,10$ give factors $1,11/7,55/28$, approaching ceiling $2.2$.
At frequency three, half/average/double length gives $1.76,1.571429,1.294118$.

### Remove Length Normalization

**New task:** Stop penalizing field length under a justified field-specific requirement.

**Picture and change:** Set $b=0$, keeping the other factors fixed.

**Check:** All three frequency-three documents now contribute $11/7$ under IDF one, regardless of their length.

**Use when:** Evaluate the changed field semantics and query slices; normalization is not universally beneficial.

### Add Semantic Coverage

**New task:** Retrieve paraphrases sharing no analyzed query token.

**Picture and change:** Compare query expansion or dense candidates, then evaluate fusion and exact-identifier protection.

**Check:** Under RRF with constant sixty, a lexical-only rank-one document scores $1/61$.
A document ranked second lexically and first densely scores $1/62+1/61$, potentially displacing it at a top-one cutoff.

**Use when:** Candidate coverage, not an assumed superiority of hybrid retrieval, is the measured bottleneck.

## TF-IDF vs BM25

| | TF saturation | Length normalization | Tunable | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **TF-IDF** | Convention-dependent; log-TF is unbounded | Optional vector normalization | Configurable weighting/analysis | Lexical vector scorer |
| **BM25** | Displayed factor tends to $k_1+1$ | Explicit via $b$ | $k_1,b$, field policies | Lexical ranking scorer |

Their scores depend on the analyzer, corpus, field statistics, and normalization policy.

## Lexical vs dense (and why hybrid wins)

| | Lexical (BM25) | Dense (embeddings) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matches | Analyzed tokens | Learned representation similarities |
| IDs/paraphrases/domain shift | Evaluate task-specific behavior | Evaluate task-specific behavior |
| Index | Typically inverted postings | Exact or approximate vector search |

Rank-based RRF and weighted-score fusion use different assumptions; raw scores are not automatically comparable across retrievers.
Reranking cannot recover candidates absent from its input set.

## What an interviewer expects you to say

Reconstruct the six bar values, name the IDF/analyzer convention, and identify whether candidate coverage or ordering needs improvement.

## Common confusions

- **Rarity as relevance.** Corpus rarity does not establish semantic importance.
- **Implicit synonym guarantees.** BM25 matches analyzed terms; explicit expansion/analyzer policies can introduce synonyms.
- **Negative-IDF confusion.** A bounded positive TF factor does not imply increasing total score when its multiplier is negative.
