## Summary

Inverted dropout randomly masks activations and scales survivors to preserve each activation's conditional expectation.
Standard evaluation replaces that random operation with identity; it does not exactly average an arbitrary nonlinear network.
Mask correlation and placement are part of the regularizer, not merely implementation details.

## The mechanism (inverted dropout)

For fixed finite $x\in\mathbb R^d$ and $0\le p<1$:

1. Sample a binary mask $m \in \{0, 1\}^d$ with $m_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(1 - p)$ independently.
2. Compute $y = m \odot x \cdot \frac{1}{1 - p}$.

Then $E[y_i\mid x]=x_i$ and $Var(y_i\mid x)=px_i^2/(1-p)$.
For the pictured $x=(1,2,3,4)$ and $p=0.5$, mask A produces $(2,0,6,0)$ and B produces $(0,4,0,8)$.
Their coordinate average is x; independently sampled masks need not arrive as complementary pairs.
For coordinate two, the random output is zero or four with equal probability: mean two, variance four.

For the counterexample $x=1$, $p=0.5$, and $f(y)=y^2$, $E[f(dropout(x))]=2$ but $f(E[dropout(x)])=1$.
Preserving E[y] therefore does not prove an exact ensemble prediction after subsequent nonlinear computation.

### Change Drop Probability

**New task:** Reduce p from one-half to one-quarter for coordinate value two.

**Picture and change:** The retained value becomes $8/3$ with probability $3/4$, rather than four with probability one-half.

**Check:** Mean remains two while variance falls from four to $4/3$.
One retained output is not the expectation.

**Use when:** Held-out behavior supports changing stochastic regularization strength; no architecture-independent rate is optimal.

### Share a Mask over a Feature Map

**New task:** Drop whole channels in a two-channel $2\times2$ activation.

**Picture and change:** Broadcast mask `[1,0]` over spatial positions at p one-half.
Channels constant at one and three become constant maps two and zero.

**Check:** Independent element masking could keep just one pixel in the second channel; this channel-shared mask cannot.
Marginal expectations still match, but correlations between masked positions differ.

**Use when:** The intended regularizer removes an entire correlated feature map.

PyTorch `nn.Dropout(p)` follows its module's train/eval mode; standard evaluation returns x without inverted scaling.

Disabling gradient recording with `no_grad` alone does not change that mode.
Evaluation mode alone does not disable gradients.

## Where to apply

| Architecture | Where dropout typically goes | Typical $p$ |
|--------------|----------------------------|------------|
| CNN | Element or channel masking at chosen sites | Tune against a matched baseline |
| Transformer | Selected attention, FFN, or residual sites | Zero is also a candidate |
| RNN | Inter-layer or explicitly time-shared masks | Match the recurrent implementation |
| Embeddings | Selected input features | Preserve task semantics |

## Variants

- **[Spatial dropout](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4280):** channel-shared masking.
- **[DropConnect](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/wan13.html):** weight masking.
- **[Variational recurrent dropout](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287):** masks shared across timesteps under its specified model.
- **[Stochastic depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09382):** residual-branch masking, with its own scaling convention.

## Relation to other regularization

Noise-induced penalties can be derived for restricted models, but dropout is not generally identical to [weight decay](/concepts/weight-decay-vs-l2/).
Normalization, augmentation, and masking can interact rather than contributing independent benefits.

## Common pitfalls

- **Mode confusion:** intentionally sampling dropout at inference differs from standard evaluation and does not automatically yield calibrated uncertainty.
- **Unsupported causality:** a smaller train/validation gap alone does not prove improved deployment quality.
- **Wrong mask units:** element, channel, timestep-shared, weight, and branch masks induce different dependencies.
