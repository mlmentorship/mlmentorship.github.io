\section*{How to use it}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Start with recent practice.} Record confirmed rounds, time available, and what you can do without notes.
  \item \textbf{Choose three gaps.} Work on the areas most likely to affect your actual loop.
  \item \textbf{Attempt, compare, retry.} Keep notes closed until the attempt ends; compare your answer with the criteria; schedule the next attempt.
  \item \textbf{Call a skill ready carefully.} Look for two successful attempts on different days and one unfamiliar follow-up.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Choose the scope before the reading}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Ask the recruiter for each round's task, duration, tools, and expected depth. A title alone does not specify the loop. Mark unconfirmed formats as questions to resolve.
  \item Attempt one representative prompt per confirmed round. Start with the weakest evidence, not your favorite subject. A score from reading is not a baseline.
  \item Reserve half your weekly time for retries, repairs, and feedback. A 40-minute design attempt plus 15-minute review uses 55 minutes, not an entire evening.
  \item Add breadth only after the diagnostic identifies a missing mechanism. Two mapped prompts sample a round; they do not prove complete coverage.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Use the same method on every route}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Attempt.} Open the diagnostic in Practice Mode and leave the answer closed. Use its timer and round-specific rubric.
  \item \textbf{Score.} Weak: cannot complete the core task. Review: workable parts, but an important gap remains. Confident: a clean timed answer with no weak rubric item. Record the exact failed step.
  \item \textbf{Repair, at most 15 minutes.} Read the relevant visual or reference section. Close it and reproduce only the missing derivation, diagram, test, or decision. If it still fails, split the repair into a smaller task.
  \item \textbf{Repeat later.} Weak: two days. Review or first clean Confident: seven days. Keep the saved date even when the answer now feels familiar. These are practice defaults, not guaranteed optimal intervals.
  \item \textbf{Transfer.} After a clean diagnostic, try the second linked prompt without notes. After two clean passes on different days, use a changed assumption in a later mixed session. Mark the pass only after doing it.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{When time runs short:} reduce new topics, not the evidence standard. An unattempted or unrepaired round remains a risk. Ask a peer to probe that round before polishing already-strong answers.

\section*{Worked routes: select, attempt, repair, transfer}
These examples show how to choose from the library. Use only the rounds in your actual loop; the browser map makes these choices from your inputs.

\subsection*{Applied Scientist: product ML, L5}
Confirmed rounds: breadth, system design, project deep-dive. Eight hours per week; four weeks.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Design diagnostic (40 min + 15 review):} \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/design-fraud-detection/?practice=1}{fraud detection}. State the decision, error costs, label delay, evaluation split, serving path, and rollback. If threshold choice is hand-waving, repair with \href{https://mlmentorship.com/review/\#decision-thresholds-asymmetric-costs-abstention}{decision thresholds and asymmetric costs}. Transfer: \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/design-ai-coding-product/?practice=1}{an AI coding product} with a different error cost and feedback loop.
  \item \textbf{Breadth diagnostic:} \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/bias-variance-tradeoff/?practice=1}{bias and variance}. Explain a learning curve without reciting definitions. Repair the \href{https://mlmentorship.com/review/\#bias-variance-of-estimators}{estimator visual}; transfer to \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/how-to-choose-loss-function/?practice=1}{loss selection}.
  \item \textbf{Project diagnostic:} \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/most-ambitious-project/?practice=1}{your ambitious project}. Use one real decision, rejected alternative, measured outcome, and failure. Transfer to \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/scope-ambiguous-problem/?practice=1}{an ambiguous scope}. Do not add coding unless it is confirmed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{ML Engineer: LLM serving, L5}
Confirmed rounds: implementation and system design. Five hours per week; two weeks.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Coding (40 + 15 min):} \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/implement-attention-from-scratch/?practice=1}{implement attention}. Test shapes, masking, numerical stability, and gradients. Repair the \href{https://mlmentorship.com/review/\#attention-mechanism}{attention visual}; transfer to \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/implement-kv-cache-decode/?practice=1}{KV-cache decoding}.
  \item \textbf{Design (40 + 15 min):} \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/design-production-llm-inference-service/?practice=1}{production LLM inference}. Quantify prefill, decode, memory, queueing, and degradation. Repair \href{https://mlmentorship.com/review/\#prefill-vs-decode}{prefill versus decode}; transfer to \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/design-ai-coding-product/?practice=1}{an AI coding product}.
  \item \textbf{Week budget:} 110 minutes for the two diagnostics; reserve 150 for retries and repairs; 40 remain. If Tuesday's coding attempt is Weak, retry Thursday. If Thursday is clean, repeat the following Thursday. Do not mark it ready on Friday from familiarity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Research Scientist: paper defense and math}
\href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/critique-ml-paper/?practice=1}{Critique a paper} for 25 minutes: separate the claim from the evidence and strongest alternative explanation. Repair \href{https://mlmentorship.com/review/\#evaluation-validity-benchmark-contamination}{evaluation validity}; transfer to \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/design-ablation-study/?practice=1}{an ablation}. For a confirmed math oral, use \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/derive-ml-math-under-pressure/?practice=1}{a 15-minute derivation}; transfer to \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/derive-logistic-regression/?practice=1}{logistic regression}. Add the \href{https://mlmentorship.com/questions/present-technical-ml-project/?practice=1}{45-minute presentation} only when that format is confirmed.

\section*{6. Readiness check}
Do not call a critical gap ready based on confidence alone.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Two successful closed-book attempts on different days.
  \item No critical scoring area remains weak.
  \item One unfamiliar follow-up or changed assumption goes well.
  \item One later mixed practice session remains workable.
  \item A peer, mentor, or interviewer has challenged at least one claim when possible.
\end{itemize}

\section*{11. Final week}
The final week is for steady practice on known gaps, not broad new coverage.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\square$ Recheck each critical round with one closed-book baseline.
  \item $\square$ Retry only the most important weak areas.
  \item $\square$ Run one mixed simulation with an unfamiliar follow-up.
  \item $\square$ Practice the 90-second project opening and three decisions.
  \item $\square$ Confirm time zone, links, format, environment, and permitted tools.
  \item $\square$ Prepare concise questions about scope, success, collaboration, and the first 90 days.
  \item $\square$ Stop adding broad topics; protect sleep and response quality.
\end{itemize}
